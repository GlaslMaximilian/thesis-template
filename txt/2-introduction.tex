\section{Ldpc Codes Fundamentals and Operation} 
\label{sec:ldpc Codes}

\gls{ldpc} codes are a class of linear error-correcting codes that were first introduced by Gallager in the 1960s \cite{Gallager}. 
They are characterized by a sparse parity-check matrix, which allows for efficient decoding algorithms. 
\gls{ldpc} codes have gained significant attention in recent years due to their near-capacity performance and suitability for modern communication systems such as 5G \gls{nr} \cite{3gpp38212}.

\section{Scope of the Thesis}
\label{sec:scope}

The scope of this thesis encompasses an extensive literature review focusing on sensitive decoding algorithms for \gls{ldpc} codes, highlighting the distinctions and comparative merits of hard versus soft decision decoding techniques. 
Additionally, a detailed exploration of the x86 processor architecture will be conducted to address critical implementation considerations, specifically evaluating whether floating-point or fixed-point data types offer greater efficiency for representing soft bits. 
Furthermore, this investigation will assess the practical utility of advanced vector instruction sets, such as \gls{avx2}, in optimizing decoding performance. 
Central to this thesis is the development of an efficient LDPC decoder implemented in C++, emphasizing both computational efficiency and decoding sensitivity. 
The performance of the decoder will be thoroughly evaluated through Monte Carlo simulations under conditions of an \gls{awgn} channel, focusing specifically on processing speed and sensitivity in decoding performance metrics.
