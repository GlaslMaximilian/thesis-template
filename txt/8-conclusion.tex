\chapter{Future Work and Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section*{Summary of Contributions}
This thesis presented the design and implementation of a \emph{core} LDPC decoder with soft-input capability. In contrast to a lab reference that operated on hard decisions, the proposed decoder processes log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) and supports four decoding algorithms: \textbf{SPA}, \textbf{MS}, \textbf{OMS}, and \textbf{NMS}. The work focused on the decoder kernel itself; other chain components---\emph{reverse scrambling}, \emph{inverse rate matching}, and \emph{CRC checking}---were treated as external modules.

Architecturally, the decoder employs iterative message passing on the Tanner graph with a layered schedule to accelerate convergence. Particular attention was paid to numerical stability in the soft domain and to a modular design of variable-node (VN) and check-node (CN) processing with compact edge message buffers.

\section*{Experimental Findings}
he performance of the implemented LDPC decoding algorithms was evaluated through Monte Carlo simulations over an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel, sweeping the signal-to-noise ratio \(E_b/N_0\) from \(-6\) to \(-4\) dB, as described in Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}. Block error rate (BLER) was used as the primary performance metric to quantify the error-correction capability of each decoding algorithm.

As detailed in Section~\ref{sec:error_correction_performance}, the Sum-Product Algorithm (SPA) consistently delivers the best error-correction performance across the evaluated SNR range. However, this high accuracy comes at the cost of significantly increased computational complexity and decoding latency, as analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:decoding_speed_complexity}.

In contrast, the Min-Sum (MS) algorithm offers the lowest computational burden and fastest processing per iteration due to its simplified check-node update rules. Nevertheless, this speed advantage is accompanied by a notable penalty in BLER performance, reflecting the trade-off between decoder complexity and error correction capability discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:decoding_algorithms}.

The Offset Min-Sum (OMS) and Normalized Min-Sum (NMS) algorithms provide an attractive middle ground, as introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:decoding_algorithms}. By incorporating an offset correction or a normalization factor in the check-node computations, OMS and NMS approach the BLER performance of SPA while incurring only a marginal increase in complexity compared to MS. This balance makes OMS and NMS especially suitable for practical deployment scenarios where decoding accuracy and computational efficiency must be jointly optimized.

In summary, the results presented throughout the thesis suggest that OMS and NMS embody a pragmatic trade-off, delivering near-optimal error correction with considerably reduced computational demands relative to SPA. This finding is consistent with theoretical expectations and practical considerations detailed in the literature and supported by the comparative analyses conducted here.

\section*{Current Limitations}
While the core decoder is functional and its behavior is well-characterized, the implementation is \emph{not yet} ready to replace the existing production decoder. Key reasons include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Missing integration with the full decoding chain (reverse scrambling, inverse rate matching, CRC-driven early stopping).
  \item Throughput and latency not yet optimized to production targets.
  \item Lack of fixed-point/quantized variants and comprehensive conformance tests across all target configurations.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Outlook and Next Steps}
With targeted engineering, the implementation can mature into a production-ready component. The following steps are recommended:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Vectorization and low-level optimization:} Introduce wide-SIMD parallelism via \textbf{AVX2} (e.g., intrinsics or guided auto-vectorization), ensure 32/64-byte alignment, adopt SoA layouts for message buffers, and favor branchless CN/VN kernels. Consider thread-level parallelism (OpenMP/TBB) across layers/codeblocks.
  \item \textbf{Quantization path:} Add fixed-point support (e.g., scaled/offset min-sum with clipping), tune OMS offsets / NMS scaling per quantization, and validate against floating-point baselines.
  \item \textbf{Chain integration:} Implement and validate reverse scrambling, inverse rate matching, and CRC checks; enable CRC-driven early termination and zero-syndrome shortcuts.
  \item \textbf{Algorithmic tuning:} Sweep OMS offset and NMS normalization across SNRs and code structures; evaluate hybrid schedules and damping to improve stability at low SNR.
  \item \textbf{Verification and robustness:} Expand unit/propagation tests, add deterministic seeds for Monte Carlo, and build regression suites covering multiple base graphs and lifting factors.
\end{itemize}

\noindent In summary, this thesis delivered a modular soft-input LDPC decoder with four decoding algorithms and demonstrated their performance trade-offs. Although not yet a drop-in replacement, the identified engineering steps---in particular \emph{AVX2}-based vectorization together with full-chain integration and quantization---provide a clear path toward a production-ready implementation.
